I am working on a research project and want your ideas, grounded in published work wherever possible. Please read the whole
context first, then answer the questions at the end. Where you cite a paper, give its title, authors/venue/year and the
specific idea we could borrow; say clearly when you are unsure a paper exists or what exactly it showed. Do not invent
results.

==================================================================
1. THE PROJECT
==================================================================

Goal: turn a choropleth map image (a map whose regions are coloured by a value, with a legend) into accurate text.
Three stages:
  1. Derenderer (a vision-language model): map image -> JSON with title, legend title, legend type (discrete or
     continuous), the legend ranges, and a value for every region (a class index for discrete legends, a number for
     continuous ones, null for no data). Regions are named by short codes (US state codes, Indian state codes).
  2. Code layer: deterministic spatial statistics on that JSON (Moran's I, LISA, Getis-Ord Gi*, trends).
  3. Narrator: an LLM turns the computed facts into prose.
This question is only about stage 1, the derenderer.

The derenderer must work for any country we target (for now the USA and India; China later), on real-world map styles,
as ONE model. The closest prior work is CMA (Butani, Sreevalsan-Nair, Kamat, IEEE GRSL 2024): a six-step pipeline
(legend classification, connected components, OCR, legend analysis, Mask R-CNN region segmentation retrained per
country, colour-to-data). Its own stated limitation is that it "could be replaced by a single model". We want that
single model, and we do NOT want to rebuild CMA's pipeline around a VLM (no shapefile fitting, OCR or legend cropping
at reading time).

Compute: Kaggle T4 GPUs (16 GB), runs of up to ~12 h; a shared lab GPU may become available.

==================================================================
2. MODEL AND DATA
==================================================================

Base model: Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct with LoRA. Its vision tower works on 14x14-pixel patches; every 2x2 patches are
merged into one 28x28-pixel token for the language model. Images up to ~640k pixels (~3,300 patches).

Data:
- MapQA (Chang et al. 2022): ~20k US state choropleths, all in one Plotly style. We test on 400 test maps (302 discrete).
- Our own synthetic generator for US and India maps (random palettes, legends, fonts, layouts) from geoBoundaries
  shapefiles, so per-pixel region labels are free.
- Real India maps: CMA's 164 India maps. 68 of them are matched to their source data tables (by title and legend
  numbers), which gives true values for scoring.

==================================================================
3. WHAT WE TRIED AND WHAT WE MEASURED
==================================================================

(a) Plain fine-tuning (image -> JSON). On MapQA: 97.8% of states right, but whole map exactly right only 33%. The
    misses are almost all tiny states (Rhode Island 47%, Delaware). On India it hardly works: on real India maps
    big states are 62% right, no better than always guessing the most common class. Printing state codes on the map
    took Indian big states from 64% to 97%, so the model does not know where Indian states are.

(b) Tiles: a few zoomed crops (3x) of the map are passed as extra images, and a small learned picker chooses where.
    On MapQA: Rhode Island 94%, whole map exact 89.7%, our best on the US. But it does not help India: US small states
    sit in one north-east cluster that one tile covers, and the picker effectively learned that. Not a general method.

(c) S2 ("scaling on scales", Shi et al. 2024), first attempt: the vision tower also ran on the map at 2x; those fine
    features were averaged back onto the 1x token grid and fused through a new 8.4M-weight linear layer. Failed
    (Rhode Island 42%): averaging back onto the coarse grid blends the tiny state with its neighbours again, and the
    new weights barely moved in ~200 updates. Generally, new modules trained only through the language-model loss
    did not learn in our budgets; things that reuse pretrained parts did.

(d) Region sheet: code fits the country's shapefile onto the map, cuts every region out and lays them out as cells on
    a separate image beside the legend; the VLM reads the cells. Trained on US only, it read synthetic India with
    100% of big states and 94.8% of small ones; real India big states 91.4%. But it needs a shapefile fit at reading
    time (CMA-like), and on MapQA it lost to tiles (74.8% vs 89.7% exact, mostly readings one shade off).

(e) Learned answer sheet (our current direction): a small conv head (~2M weights) on the vision tower's 14-px patch
    features predicts, for every patch, which region it belongs to, out of a fixed list of 87 classes (background,
    50 US states, 36 Indian regions). Summing the head's belief by country gives the country (100% right on every
    test set). Each region's patch features are averaged, weighted by the head's belief squared, and passed through
    Qwen's own 2x2 merger into ONE token per region. The language model reads "code: [region token]" for every
    region, plus the normal map tokens, and writes the JSON. Shapefiles are used only to make per-patch training
    labels, never at reading time. Half the training maps use random made-up region codes and random region subsets,
    so the reader copies colours rather than recalling names.
    v1 results: real India big states 93.2% (best so far), small regions 72.3%; synthetic India big 99.9%;
    MapQA exact 32.5%, Rhode Island 50.7%.

(f) Diagnosis of small regions: it is resolution, not too little training. Delhi and Rhode Island cover about 0.2 of
    one 14-px patch; even with perfect labels their pooled token can be at most 14-57% their own pixels; accuracy
    follows region area almost exactly (Delhi 63%, Goa 88%, Sikkim 97%, Tripura 99%).

(g) v2: zoom inside the answer sheet. For each region the head finds fewer than 3 patches of, crop around where the
    head put it, enlarge 3x, run the tower and head on the crop, and pool that region's token from the zoomed view.
    The head and the reader are both trained on zoomed views (zooming without training failed).
    v2 vs v1:
      MapQA whole map exact      32.5% -> 70.2%   (tiles: 89.7%)
      MapQA Rhode Island         50.7% -> 73.8%   (tiles: 94.4%)
      MapQA Delaware             73.5% -> 94.7%
      Synthetic India small      89.2% -> 93.3%   (Delhi 68% -> 84%)
      Real India small           72.3% -> 76.1%   (region sheet: 85.2%)
      Real India big             93.2% -> 89.5%   (not significant: 8 maps better, 8 worse)
      Real India continuous error, median 9.3% -> 4.5%, but two maps blew up
    What v2's own logs show:
      - Finding fails: the head decides where to zoom from the 1x grid, where Delhi is a fifth of a patch. Delhi was
        never zoomed on 58 of 68 real maps (the head's peak belief was below 5% everywhere); Rhode Island was never
        zoomed on 169 of 400 MapQA maps.
      - 3x is not enough: after zooming, Rhode Island, Delhi and Andaman tokens are still only 56-69% their own pixels
        (others 77-92%).
      - It zooms regions that are not small: on real maps medium states get zoomed and their tokens get worse
        (Himachal 74% -> 23% pure).
      - On a few real maps the whole map shifts by one class or more (every lowest-class state read as class 4),
        apparently when many region tokens are replaced by zoomed ones.

(h) Running now (v3), two variants from v2's weights:
      - S2 done per region: the map enlarged 2x, cut into 2x2 tiles of the original size, run through the tower, and
        the patch grids stitched into one grid at twice the detail (Delhi ~0.8 patch). The head reads that grid and
        every region token is pooled from it. No averaging back onto the coarse grid and no new layers before the LLM.
      - The same, plus a zoom sized per region (2x-8x, so a region covers ~8 patches), with the zoomed token kept
        only if the head's belief in the region rises.

==================================================================
4. CONSTRAINTS
==================================================================

- One model at reading time: no shapefile fitting, no OCR, no hand-written legend parsing at inference.
- Shapefiles (and our synthetic generator) are fine for making training labels.
- It must generalise across countries and map styles; the fixed 87-class head is a known limitation (a new country
  means new head outputs and a short training round).
- Fits a 3B VLM on a 16 GB GPU, training budgets of a few hours.
- New modules that only get gradient through the language-model loss have not learned in our budgets; dense
  per-patch supervision has worked well.

==================================================================
5. QUESTIONS
==================================================================

1. Small regions. What published methods address objects or regions far smaller than one ViT patch, in VLMs or in
   segmentation/detection, that fit our setup? We already know: "MLLMs Know Where to Look" (crop by attention),
   V* / SEAL guided visual search, ZoomEye, FeatUp (feature upsampling), S2 scaling, superpixel and sub-object
   tokenisation (SPiT, subobject-level tokens), Osprey and Groma (region tokens). Which of these, or others, would you
   use, and how exactly would you adapt them? Is there a better way to FIND a sub-patch region before zooming than
   peak belief on a coarse grid (e.g. objectness, boundary/edge maps, coarse-to-fine search, using neighbouring
   regions as anchors)?

2. Class-agnostic regions. Our head is tied to a fixed list of regions. Are there published ways to make the
   "which pixels belong to one region" step class-agnostic (e.g. instance grouping, contrastive pixel embeddings,
   SAM-style prompting, slot attention), and attach names afterwards, so a new country needs little or no retraining?
   What would you use to name the regions after grouping, without a shapefile at reading time?

3. Colour-to-legend reading. Many remaining errors are "one shade off" and occasional whole-map shifts by one class.
   Are there published ideas for reading a value from a colour against a legend reliably in VLMs (chart/plot
   derendering such as DePlot, MatCha, ChartQA work, colour-bar reading, explicit legend-swatch tokens, ordinal or
   relative comparison losses)? Would giving the legend swatches their own tokens, so each region token is compared
   with swatch tokens, be supported by any prior work?

4. The whole-map shifts. Why might replacing some region tokens with tokens made from zoomed views shift the class of
   regions that were not zoomed? Is there known work on keeping token statistics consistent across scales (e.g.
   scale-consistency losses, feature normalisation across resolutions)?

5. Overall direction. Given everything above, which direction is most likely to give a strong, publishable single
   model for choropleth derendering across countries? What is novel here relative to CMA, MapQA, MAPWise and general
   chart-derendering work, and what ablations or baselines would a reviewer expect?

Please be concrete: for each suggestion say what to change in our model, what labels or training it needs, roughly
how expensive it is, and how we would know quickly (a cheap test) whether it works.
